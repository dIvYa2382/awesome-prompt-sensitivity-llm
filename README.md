## Overview

Prompt sensitivity refers to the dependence of an LLM's output on the exact wording, structure, ordering, or contextual information contained in a prompt. Two prompts that communicate essentially the same meaning can sometimes produce different answers, reasoning paths, confidence levels, or conclusions. This phenomenon is important when LLMs are used for research because unstable outputs can affect the reproducibility and reliability of research conclusions.

Prompt sensitivity can arise from several factors, including wording changes, prompt templates, few-shot demonstrations, demonstration ordering, answer-label biases, reasoning instructions, and contextual information. Research on in-context learning has shown that the choice and order of demonstrations can substantially influence model performance. Other work has proposed calibration, self-consistency, prompt optimization, and structured reasoning methods to reduce or understand this variability.

For research applications, the problem is particularly important because an LLM-generated conclusion may appear convincing while changing when the same evidence is presented using a different prompt. Therefore, researchers should evaluate conclusions across multiple semantically equivalent prompts rather than relying on a single prompt-output pair.

This repository organizes research on prompt sensitivity into several themes: foundational LLM research, prompt and in-context learning, prompt sensitivity and robustness, reasoning methods, evaluation benchmarks, datasets, tools, and reproducible implementations.

# Survey and Review Papers

### 1. A Systematic Survey of Prompt Engineering in Large Language Models: Techniques and Applications

**Sahoo et al. (2024)**

A broad survey covering prompt engineering techniques, applications, datasets, models, strengths, and limitations.

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07927)

### 2. A Survey of Prompt Engineering Methods in Large Language Models for Different NLP Tasks

**Vatsal and Dubey (2024)**

Reviews prompting techniques across multiple NLP tasks and datasets.

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12994)

### 3. Towards Goal-oriented Prompt Engineering for Large Language Models: A Survey

**Li, Leung and Shen (2024)**

Examines goal-oriented prompt formulation and organizes prompting methods according to stages of task solving.

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14043)

### 4. A Comprehensive Taxonomy of Prompt Engineering Techniques for Large Language Models

**Liu et al. (2025)**

Provides a taxonomy covering instruction, knowledge, reasoning/planning, and reliability-oriented prompting.

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11704-025-50058-z)

# Foundational Papers

### 5. Language Models are Few-Shot Learners

**Brown et al. (2020), NeurIPS**

The GPT-3 paper demonstrating strong task performance using natural-language prompts and few-shot demonstrations without gradient updates.

[Paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/1457c0d6bfcb4967418bfb8ac142f64a-Abstract.html)

### 6. Making Pre-trained Language Models Better Few-shot Learners

**Gao, Fisch and Chen (2021), ACL-IJCNLP**

Introduces LM-BFF and studies prompt-based few-shot learning and demonstration selection.

[Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.295/)

### 7. Rethinking the Role of Demonstrations: What Makes In-Context Learning Work?

**Min et al. (2022), EMNLP**

Studies which aspects of demonstrations influence in-context learning and shows that formatting and input distribution can matter substantially.

[Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.759/)

### 8. Calibrate Before Use: Improving Few-shot Performance of Language Models

**Zhao et al. (2021), ICML**

Demonstrates that prompt format, examples, and example order can cause substantial instability and proposes contextual calibration.

[Paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/zhao21c.html)

# Prompt Sensitivity and Stability

### 9. Fantastically Ordered Prompts and Where to Find Them: Overcoming Few-Shot Prompt Order Sensitivity

**Lu et al. (2022), ACL**

Shows that changing the order of few-shot examples can produce large changes in model performance.

[Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.556/)

### 10. Flaw or Artifact? Rethinking Prompt Sensitivity in Evaluating LLMs

**Hua et al. (2025), EMNLP**

Investigates whether measured prompt sensitivity partly results from evaluation procedures such as rigid answer matching and log-likelihood scoring.

[Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1006/)

### 11. Understanding the Prompt Sensitivity

**Liu et al. (2026), ACL**

Provides a theoretical analysis of prompt sensitivity by examining LLMs as multivariate functions and studying the relationship between meaning-preserving prompts and model probabilities.

[Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.2053/)

### 12. How Many Demonstrations Do You Need for In-context Learning?

**Chen et al. (2023), Findings of EMNLP**

Investigates the number and usefulness of demonstrations required for in-context learning.

[Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.745/)

# Prompting and Reasoning Methods

### 13. Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models

**Wei et al. (2022), NeurIPS**

Shows that providing intermediate reasoning examples can improve performance on complex reasoning tasks.

[Paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/9d5609613524ecf4f15af0f7b31abca4-Abstract-Conference.html)

### 14. Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Reasoners

**Kojima et al. (2022), NeurIPS**

Demonstrates that a simple zero-shot reasoning instruction can significantly improve performance on several reasoning benchmarks.

[Paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2022/hash/8bb0d291acd4acf06ef112099c16f326-Abstract-Conference.html)

### 15. Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models

**Wang et al. (2023), ICLR**

Introduces self-consistency by sampling multiple reasoning paths and selecting the most consistent answer.

[Paper](https://research.google/pubs/self-consistency-improves-chain-of-thought-reasoning-in-language-models/)

### 16. Least-to-Most Prompting Enables Complex Reasoning in Large Language Models

**Zhou et al. (2023), ICLR**

Breaks complex problems into simpler subproblems and solves them sequentially.

[Paper](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2023/zhou2023iclr-leasttomost/)

### 17. Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models

**Yao et al. (2023), NeurIPS**

Extends chain-of-thought reasoning by exploring multiple reasoning paths and enabling deliberate search and backtracking.

[Paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/271db9922b8d1f4dd7aaef84ed5ac703-Abstract.html)

### 18. Prompt Programming for Large Language Models: Beyond the Few-Shot Paradigm

**Reynolds and McDonell (2021)**

Studies prompt programming and argues that zero-shot prompting can sometimes outperform few-shot prompting.

[Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07350)

**Lester, Al-Rfou and Constant (2021), EMNLP**

Studies soft prompt tuning and demonstrates how prompt tuning becomes increasingly effective as model scale increases.

[Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.243/)

# Research Themes

The resources in this repository can be organized into the following major themes:

1. **Prompt wording sensitivity** — changes in wording can influence model outputs.
2. **Few-shot demonstration sensitivity** — different examples can produce different results.
3. **Demonstration-order sensitivity** — changing example order can alter performance.
4. **Prompt-template sensitivity** — formatting and instruction structure can affect outputs.
5. **Reasoning-prompt sensitivity** — reasoning instructions such as chain-of-thought can change performance.
6. **Evaluation sensitivity** — some apparent instability may be caused by the evaluation method itself.
7. **Prompt optimization** — automated methods can search for more effective prompts.
8. **Output consistency** — methods such as self-consistency can reduce variation across reasoning paths.
9. **Research reproducibility** — prompt versions should be recorded so that experiments can be reproduced.
10. **Citation and factual reliability** — generated research claims should be checked against authoritative sources.

# Citation Integrity

This repository follows a verification-first approach.

Before including a research paper, the following should be checked:

* Correct title
* Correct authors
* Correct publication year
* Correct journal or conference
* DOI or persistent identifier where available
* Existence of the paper
* Whether the link actually points to the cited paper
* Whether the paper supports the claim for which it is cited

AI tools can help discover candidate papers, but references should be independently verified before inclusion.
